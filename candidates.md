# Candidates — "Describe Your Ex in One Word" (u-puan-describe-your-ex-in-one-word-9fc6ebe7)

Manual selection only. No Gemini judge, no NVIDIA judge, no judge-API/proxy tournament.

Ranked against the kit judge brief's criteria IN THEIR STATED ORDER:

1. One word that describes an ex, instantly readable as a verdict on a former partner.
2. Comic punch: lands as a joke at a glance, through a twist, an unexpected angle or a precise absurdity — not a plain insult.
3. Surprise: not the obvious choice; startles while staying instantly clear.
4. Craft: exactly one word, spelled correctly, no punctuation tricks, meaning arrives without a caption.
5. Recognition: anyone who has had an ex feels the truth in it, so the laugh comes with a nod.

## The candidates

1. **cameo** — He was a cameo: a brief guest appearance in the movie of my life, in and out before the plot noticed.
2. **intern** — He was an intern: temporary, unpaid, still learning on the job, never a real hire.
3. **unsubscribe** — He was an unsubscribe: the spam I finally opted out of.
4. **refund** — He was a refund: a defective purchase I sent back and want my time back for.
5. **detour** — He was a detour: a wrong turn that took me miles off my route.
6. **audition** — He was an audition: he tried out for the lead role and did not get the part.
7. **expired** — He expired, like milk past its date: fine once, then suddenly not.
8. **loaner** — He was a loaner: a temporary replacement while the real thing was in the shop.
9. **leftovers** — He was leftovers: reheated, and nobody wanted seconds.
10. **downgrade** — He was a downgrade: the version of my life that ran worse than the one before.
11. **placeholder** — He was a placeholder: holding the seat until the real one arrived.
12. **tourist** — He was a tourist: just passing through, took photos, left.
13. **curdled** — He curdled: went off quietly in the back of the fridge.
14. **void** — He was void: null and void, like a cancelled contract.

## Manual ranking (brief criteria in order, then pairwise)

**Winner: cameo.** Criterion 1: a cameo IS a person who appears briefly — the verdict reads instantly as "he was only ever a brief appearance in my life". Criterion 2: the film-frame twist is a genuine unexpected angle, not an insult; the joke arrives with the word itself. Criterion 3: nobody's obvious pick for an ex. Criterion 4: five letters, one clean word, zero tricks. Criterion 5: everyone has had a brief-chapter person — the nod is universal.

**Runner-up: intern.** Just as instant and arguably punchier on criterion 2 (unpaid, still learning, temporary contract — a precise absurdity). It loses to cameo on criterion 1's clarity: "intern" can be read as merely "he was young", which dulls the verdict, while "cameo" has exactly one reading — brief appearance. Cameo is also shorter (5 vs 6 chars), which the tie-break favours.

**The rest, in order:**

3. **unsubscribe** — modern, high recognition, strong surprise. Loses to the top two on criterion 1: it names an action (opting out), not the person, so the verdict is one step removed.
4. **refund** — strong punch ("I want my time back") and universal. Same flaw as unsubscribe: "a refund" describes the transaction, not the ex himself.
5. **detour** — instant, universal, funny. But a detour is a fairly common metaphor for a bad relationship phase, so it scores lower on surprise than the top four.
6. **audition** — same film family as cameo, strong twist. Loses to cameo head-to-head: an audition implies he never got the role, but he WAS the ex — he did get it, briefly. Cameo's internal logic is cleaner.
7. **expired** — instant and universal, but leans toward a plain insult ("he's done") and carries a faintly dark second reading; less twist than the top six.
8. **loaner** — vivid and surprising, but recognition depends on knowing loaner cars; narrower than the words above it.
9. **leftovers** — funny and universal, but blunter and more insult-leaning than the top eight; the laugh is at him, not at the angle.
10. **downgrade** — a good tech metaphor, but it judges the relationship's outcome more than the person; weaker on criterion 1 than the words above.
11. **placeholder** — stings well, but earnest and a little long; the punch is a slow burn rather than a glance.
12. **tourist** — "just passing through" is charming, but a common metaphor for a fling; moderate surprise.
13. **curdled** — vivid and unexpected, but the milk metaphor needs half a beat and leans gross; weaker on criterion 4 (meaning without a caption).
14. **void** — short, dark, punchy, but ambiguous: emptiness vs a voided contract. The reader has to supply the contract reading, so it is the weakest on criterion 4.

## Pairwise vs the obvious rival shapes

- vs **toxic**: every candidate above beats it — "toxic" is a plain insult with zero twist and zero surprise; it is the first word anyone writes.
- vs **cheater**: same — a factual accusation, not a joke; no angle, no absurdity.
- vs **ghost**: "ghost" has a small twist (he vanished), but it is the single most predictable ex word in the field; "cameo" beats it on surprise and on punch (a cameo chose to appear briefly; a ghost just left).
- vs **narcissist**: a clinical label, not a joke; loses on criteria 2 and 3 outright to any word in the top six.

## Final choice

**cameo** — 5 characters, 5 bytes. One word, spelled correctly, no punctuation, the joke self-contained.
